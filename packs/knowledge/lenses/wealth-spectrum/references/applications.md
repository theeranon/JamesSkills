# Wealth Spectrum Applications

## Evidence required first

- Current net cash flow, liquidity, debt pressure, and runway.
- Revenue quality, gross margin, recurrence, and concentration.
- Work and decisions that still return to the owner.
- Team ownership, operating rhythm, measures, and repeatability.
- Assets or systems that produce value without continuous personal effort.

## Core questions

1. What is the present constraint: survival, earned income, market mastery, enterprise execution, or capital orchestration?
2. Which observable facts support that hypothesis, and which facts contradict it?
3. Is the business genuinely producing positive flow, or merely showing revenue while liquidity and owner load deteriorate?
4. What capability must become reliable before a higher-stage strategy is safe?
5. Which lower-stage discipline must remain intact during the transition?

## Reversible experiments

### Owner-dependence test

For 14 days, name one recurring result, its non-owner accountable person, definition of done, and review metric. Measure how many decisions and rework loops return to the owner. Keep the stage hypothesis only if the intervention exposes the predicted bottleneck.

### Operating-rhythm test

Run one weekly metrics review and one explicit handoff loop for four weeks. Measure missed commitments, cycle time, and owner rescues. If results do not improve, test a competing explanation such as weak demand, insufficient capability, or unclear authority.

### Financial-reality test

Recalculate the stage hypothesis from current cash flow, liabilities, and owner dependence after a material business-model or liquidity change. Never preserve a color because it feels aspirational.

## Decision output

Return: provisional stage or `unknown`, current evidence, contradiction, most likely constraint, next capability, experiment, metric, duration, and revision trigger.
