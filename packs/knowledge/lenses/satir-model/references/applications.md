# Applications

## SM-101 — Enter at behaviour, contract, then descend

- Evidence: `[official claim]`
- Source: `banmen-satir-yesterday-today-2002#session`
- Confidence: `source-faithful`

Take the person's story as context only and keep it short. Banmen's own criticism of practitioners is that they ask content questions instead of process questions and so stay in talk therapy. Set a positively directional goal for each internal level rather than for behaviour alone, get an explicit commitment to work on it, then work experientially, then anchor what shifted.

The transferable contracting move for a coaching frame is to name the framework and ask permission, scoped to one behaviour the person has already said they want to change. Descend from the top down; the institute's own question material is ordered that way.

## SM-102 — The coaching sequence ends in a rehearsed commitment

- Evidence: `[inference]`
- Source: `banmen-satir-yesterday-today-2002#session`, `icf-referring-client-to-therapy-2018#explore-past`
- Confidence: `supported`

Observed practitioner sequence: behaviour, feeling, feeling about the feeling, trigger, value, yearning, then construct a new belief, check how it lands in the body, stress-test it against the original provocation, re-check the feeling, and have the person summarise in their own words.

The stress-test is what separates this from a generic values conversation. The close is what keeps it coaching rather than therapy: name the next concrete action, rehearse it aloud in the room, check the feeling of the rehearsal, and contract a follow-up review.

## SM-103 — Business and leadership use runs structure-only

- Evidence: `[independent evidence]`
- Source: `banmen-satir-yesterday-today-2002#entry`
- Confidence: `mixed`

The practitioner with the most documented executive delivery describes a structure-only method for organisational work, without personal disclosure. Treat that as the default posture for any work-performance or business-owner conversation, not as a caveat.

Reported leadership failure modes map onto the stances: the placating leader absorbs subordinates' problems and swallows their own grievance; the blaming leader attacks the high-expectation low-performer and regrets it afterwards; the super-reasonable leader is principled, organised, and experienced as having no human warmth; the distracting leader defuses conflict by changing the subject so pressure accumulates. These mappings are asserted by practitioners; no outcome study supports them.

## SM-104 — Stop and refer on daily functioning

- Evidence: `[official claim]`
- Source: `icf-referring-client-to-therapy-2018#four-conditions`, `icf-referring-client-to-therapy-2018#parallel`
- Confidence: `source-faithful`

A coach may explore the past in service of understanding the story being told about the present, but does not linger there. If acknowledging it is enough for the person to move forward, continue. If it needs deeper processing, refer.

Refer when the matter is outside your competence, interferes with daily functioning, blocks progress in the coaching, or is psychological in nature. Level of daily functioning is the primary indicator. Watch for marked mood change, decline in work performance, social withdrawal, changes in sleep or self-care, and hopelessness.

Parallel working is permitted. The procedure is to say the role is changing, explain the distinction, ask permission, refer, and then explicitly resume coaching. The session is not abandoned.

Three further rules taken from practitioner writing rather than any institute: do not reach for the iceberg when a lighter tool would do; ask whose interest the descent serves; and if emotion surges, stabilise first and refer second.

## Reversible experiments

Every application ends in something the person can try and undo. Never a diagnosis, never a label.

- Name the stance you fall into with one specific person this week, and write down which of the four entry points would have reached you.
- Take one recurring reaction and write the expectation underneath it, then ask that person directly whether the expectation is even true.
- Run one conversation you have been avoiding, using the entry point that matches their stance rather than your own.
- Rehearse one sentence aloud before the meeting where it is needed, and note afterwards whether saying it changed the outcome.

Each carries a success rule and a revision rule stated before it starts. If the experiment changes nothing, drop the lens rather than reaching for a deeper level.
