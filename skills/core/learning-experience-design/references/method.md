# Learning Experience Design Method

This is the portable form of James's general Learning Experience Design operating discipline. It is broader than the SolutionsIMPACT e-learning product about knowledge transfer and may use several house frameworks.

## 1. Authority And Classification

Use sources in this order:

1. newest direct correction or accepted decision
2. confirmed client promise and source evidence
3. current course source of truth or accepted design
4. current delivery constraints and learner evidence
5. reusable patterns and prior debriefs
6. older decks and drafts as supporting evidence only

Classify the request before designing:

- `minor`: presentation details change while learner, promise, and learning logic remain fixed
- `major`: learner, use case, outcome, agenda, or artifact changes
- `new course`: no accepted learning spine exists
- `journey`: organization-level change requires several interventions, sponsor decisions, governance, and phase-to-phase evidence

Use the macro-journey workflow for the journey class. Do not disguise an organization transformation as a long agenda or import an offer-specific phase model without authority.

Duration alone does not decide the route. A multi-day course remains LED when it owns one bounded learner or capability movement; a short session remains a child of a macro journey when the accountable outcome is organization transformation.

Create, audit, repair, and improve are internal execution states. Infer the state from the request and available artifacts; never require the user to name it.

## 2. Reality Snapshot

Capture what is known about:

- client or project and authoritative source
- business tension and why now
- learner roles, current capability, count, language, and context
- observable learner A and B
- date, duration, location, format, and delivery conditions
- confirmed promise and deliverables
- trainer, TA, producer, and decision owner
- available assets and their authority
- constraints, risks, deadline, and commercial reality

Unknown fields do not automatically stop the work. Mark what they affect, continue the reversible design, and request only the decision that changes the next step.

## 3. Seven Readiness Gates

1. Client scope: source proof and promise agree.
2. Learner reality: role, level, count, and room type are known enough to design.
3. Learning outcome: A, B, artifact, and visible success evidence are coherent.
4. Reusable pattern: a relevant pattern exists or new logic is explicitly being created.
5. Asset certainty: current source assets and production owner are identifiable.
6. Trainer and TA readiness: the delivery team can explain, support, and recover the planned experience.
7. Economics and feasibility: time, effort, risk, and value fit the assignment.

Report each gate as ready, provisional, or blocked by an exact decision. A missing optional asset is not permission to stop independent design work.

## 4. Derive The Learning Spine

Use this sequence without skipping ahead:

```text
real tension
-> learner or role being transformed
-> current observable state A
-> barrier or misconception
-> future observable state B
-> credible evidence
-> learner artifact
-> learning sequence
-> delivery assets
```

The artifact is selected after the evidence. A beautiful canvas, deck, or workbook cannot rescue a weak learner transformation.

Trace evidence through three states:

```text
Produced
-> Applied
-> Impact Proven
```

Satisfaction, confidence, and presentation polish are diagnostic breadcrumbs. They do not replace work application or organizational impact.

Write the promise so it names a useful movement rather than a topic list. State a boundary to prevent the course from promising an organization-wide result it cannot own.

## 5. Design The Experience

Map three horizons:

- Before: prerequisite, context, motivation, baseline, and readiness
- During: experience, explanation, comparison, practice, decision, and feedback
- After: retrieval cue, work application, peer or manager support, and evidence

For every module or time block, specify:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| Before state | What can the learner not yet see, decide, or do? |
| Trigger | What creates attention, relevance, or productive tension? |
| Principle | What idea improves the learner's judgment? |
| Example or evidence | What makes the principle concrete and credible? |
| Practice | What real decision or action must the learner attempt? |
| Output | What does the learner produce or change? |
| Evidence | What proves movement rather than participation? |
| After state | What can the learner now see, decide, or do? |
| Transfer | Where and how will this survive outside the room? |

Use one continuous case when it improves causal understanding. Change cases only when the contrast itself teaches something necessary.

## 6. Facilitation And Recovery

For each meaningful time block, record:

```text
learner state
teaching intent
facilitator move
expected signal
fallback
time cost
```

The course is delivery-ready when trainer and TA can handle expected questions, room states, tool failures, and timing pressure. Slide completion is only an asset milestone.

Useful sequencing principles:

- establish why before instruction
- create a hook before a concept ladder
- show a worked example or flow before asking learners to build
- protect the learner's final usable output over decorative activities
- provide support and a non-tool fallback in tool-heavy rooms

## 7. Output And Handoff Contract

Account for every requested topic or requirement before compressing the design:

| Requested item | Authority or source | Disposition | Causal reason | Where handled |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| topic, tool, activity, or deliverable | confirmed, inferred, or conflicting | keep, transform, defer, or reject | how it supports or distracts from A to B | module, appendix, follow-up, or decision gap |

This ledger protects both sides of the design decision. Do not silently drop a client request, and do not preserve a low-value topic merely because it appeared in the brief.

The design must let another capable trainer or production team answer:

- who changes, from what to what, and why it matters
- what each segment causes
- what learners produce and how it is judged
- what to prepare, display, print, or configure
- what evidence to capture
- what to do when the room or tool behaves differently
- what remains provisional and who decides it

After delivery, capture room state, what worked, what broke, the observed signal, the move used, the result, and the reusable rule. Update the pattern rather than leaving the learning in chat.

## 8. Protected Boundaries

- Learning Experience Design is `LED` in James's operating vocabulary.
- The e-learning product called Learning Experience Design focuses on knowledge transfer and organizational assets. Use it as a specialized pattern only when that is the actual job.
- Transformative Productivity System is one SolutionsIMPACT house framework available inside Learning Experience Design. It is not LED itself and is not mandatory for every learning experience.
- Select the smallest fitting house design authority from the maintained library before using outside research. When framework-level logic is required, the primary framework must be house-owned. Research supports or challenges the selected authority; it does not silently become company IP.
- Preserve registry object types and lifecycle. An approved house principle or pattern may guide a reversible design skeleton without becoming a framework. A candidate or source-gap asset may inform only a clearly labeled provisional skeleton and cannot satisfy the delivery-ready gate.
- Older material described Learning Experience Design as one TPS lever. The newest owner decision supersedes that hierarchy. Preserve older material as historical evidence, not an active alternative.
- The exact approved TPS component names remain unresolved. Preserve the source gap rather than inventing labels.
- A macro transformation journey may use LED and TPS for each bounded intervention after its own approved architecture is locked. The SolutionsIMPACT AI Transformation Journey five-phase offer is one reference pattern, not a universal LED law.

If no approved house framework fits a recurring method gap, route the gap to `build-framework`. Never create a named framework inside one client course.
