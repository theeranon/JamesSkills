# Transformation Journey

Own the macro architecture of an organization or cohort transformation. A journey is not a list of courses. It connects the starting reality to real work, several interventions, application, evidence, leadership decisions, and measurable organizational impact.

Read [references/ai-transformation-five-phase.md](references/ai-transformation-five-phase.md) only when the sourced AI offer or an explicitly approved five-phase pattern is in scope. Use [assets/transformation-journey-blueprint.html](assets/transformation-journey-blueprint.html) for the final report.

## Framework use

Search the SolutionsIMPACT house library first. The five-phase transformation pattern is the preferred house candidate because it recurs in current practice; its current lifecycle is pilot, and it is not a universal law or universally approved company law. Its complete contract is verified for bounded use in the AI Transformation offer. Leadership, Business, Trainer, and other offers may use different phase definitions until their own evidence and approval exist.

External theories and research may support a house journey authority. Preserve whether the selected asset is a framework, pattern, method, or principle. If framework-level logic is required and no approved house framework fits, use `build-framework`; do not improvise a named phase model inside the client journey.

## Workflow

1. Establish authority, contract, sponsor, why now, organization or cohort A and B, business outcome, baseline, and final evidence.
2. Infer the internal operation from the request: create, audit, repair, or improve. Do not ask the user to choose this label.
3. Select the approved domain and offer framework, or a lifecycle-bounded candidate when the job is explicitly inside its evidenced pilot scope. Disclose kind, lifecycle, source, version, fit, permitted use, and unresolved gaps.
4. Lock the macro architecture before writing daily agendas. Every phase needs purpose, input, intervention, output, evidence, owner, exit condition, and handoff.
5. Trace `Produced -> Applied -> Impact Proven` from real project work to organizational or business results. Satisfaction remains a breadcrumb only.
6. Use `learning-experience-design` for each bounded workshop day, course, session, or learning intervention. The child design may improve its experience but may not rewrite the macro outcome, phase responsibilities, governance, or evidence contract.
7. Design the system around learning: diagnosis, sponsor alignment, project tracking, coaching, implementation, adaptation, risk escalation, asset ownership, and decision reviews.
8. Account for every accepted promise as mapped, transformed, deferred, or rejected with source, owner, evidence, and destination.
9. Produce one executive HTML Journey Pack and the intervention briefs required for LED.
10. Capture post-journey evidence and send reusable method improvements to `build-framework` as candidates.

If sources are incomplete, finish the reversible Journey Skeleton and name the exact decision gap. Never invent a client promise, business metric, phase model, or impact claim.

## Ready gate

The journey is ready only when every phase has accountable ownership, evidence, and a handoff; intervention outputs feed the next phase; real application and coaching move work toward implementation; the final evidence supports an organizational decision; LED children pass their own gate; and no unsupported framework or promise is presented as approved.
