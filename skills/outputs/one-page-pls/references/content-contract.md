# Content Contract

## Topic boundary

A topic is a bounded subject with its own outcome, decision, owner, action path, or timeline. Use explicit agenda labels when the source provides them. Otherwise detect natural boundaries from changes in those elements.

- One topic produces one file.
- Two or more topics produce the same number of separately named files.
- A file must make sense without opening another topic's file.
- Shared meeting context may be repeated briefly where needed for self-containment.
- Closely related supporting points may stay together; independent decisions may not.

## Lossless compression gate

Build a source inventory before drafting. Account for every material item as one of:

1. visible on the one-page artifact
2. retained in a clearly linked appendix or full record
3. excluded with an explicit, defensible reason because it does not affect the topic

The one-page artifact must preserve, when present:

- the topic and decision or current conclusion
- facts, exact numbers, and evidence that support it
- actions with owner and date or completion condition
- risks, constraints, dependencies, and fallback
- uncertainty, source limitation, or unresolved decision

Keep fact, inference, and recommendation distinguishable. Never invent a quote, owner, deadline, approval, or certainty. Preserve exact names, figures, and dates from the source.

## Fit decision

Compress wording and remove repetition before removing substance. If all material content cannot fit at readable size:

- keep the decision surface on one page and link a companion appendix or full record; or
- state that the topic is unsuitable for a truthful one-page artifact.

Never solve overflow by mixing topics, silently dropping a material item, hiding content, clipping it, or reducing type below the QA minimum.

## Recommended information order

1. topic title and context
2. decision or current conclusion
3. supporting evidence
4. actions, owners, and dates
5. risks and controls
6. source status and linked detail

The exact layout may change when the content demands it. The semantic contract may not.
