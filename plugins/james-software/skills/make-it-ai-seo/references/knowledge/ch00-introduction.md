# ch00 — Introduction

## Core idea
- Search is now split between classic engines (Google, Bing) and AI answer tools (ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, Google AI Mode). Optimization must cover both.
- AI SEO = the work of helping search engines and AI systems understand, retrieve, cite, and recommend a brand accurately. Traditional SEO remains the foundation; AI SEO extends it.
- The method is a repeatable loop, not a trick: understand how search works now, measure current visibility, make pages easy to retrieve and quote, strengthen credibility off-site, then iterate on evidence.

## Terminology to keep consistent
- AI platform: the product a person uses (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, Copilot, Google AI Mode).
- AI system: the whole pipeline behind an answer (models + retrieval + search indexes + knowledge sources + interface).
- Use "platform" when discussing where users interact; "system" when discussing how an answer is generated.

## What the method promises (and does not)
- Promises: a baseline audit of brand presence in AI answers, tracking of coverage, position, share of voice, citations and classic SEO metrics, pages that answer buyer questions clearly, fixes for retrieval/technical problems, consistent brand data across the web, and responsible use (accuracy, privacy, compliance).
- Does not promise: appearing in every AI answer, overnight results, or any hack. Treat any "guaranteed AI visibility" claim as a red flag.

## Reader assumptions (useful when scoping an audit)
- The client/site owner knows basic SEO terms, uses or is willing to test AI tools, wants practical steps, and cares about leads, sales, trust and accurate representation.
- No coding or model-training knowledge is needed; explain technical items in plain language in any deliverable.

## Book structure (map for choosing the next file)
- Part 1 (ch01-ch04): what AI SEO is, how search works now, first 30 days, vocabulary.
- Part 2 (ch05-ch08): measurement: prompts, coverage, position, SoV, dashboards, competitors, citations, experiments.
- Part 3 (ch09-ch15): onsite foundation: topics, AI-readiness, HTML/architecture, bottom-of-funnel pages, topical authority, underperformers, media and data.
- Part 4 (ch16-ch20): offsite footprint: signals, profiles, topic ownership, reviews, links/mentions/citations.
- Part 5 (ch21 plus playbooks): governance, risk, and business-model playbooks (local, e-commerce, B2B, SaaS, publisher).
- Part 6 (ch22-ch23): myths and creative techniques.
- Companion downloads referenced by the book: Cheat Sheet (quick-start checklist, terms, measurement reminders), templates (prompt-tracking sheet, evidence log, SoV tracker, weekly report), sample code, and a business-type playbook. Obtain from the publisher site if needed; do not assume access.

## Where to start by situation
- Brand-new to AI SEO: ch01 then ch03.
- Want to know current visibility: Part 2 (ch05 onward).
- Want to improve the website: Part 3.
- Messy profiles, reviews, mentions: Part 4.
- Regulated or risky niche, or choosing a playbook: Part 5.
- Need quick reminders: ch04 glossary, ch22, ch23.

## Audit checks
- [ ] The audit states which AI platforms are in scope and which classic engines are in scope.
- [ ] The audit separates "platform-level" findings (how the answer is displayed) from "system-level" findings (what is retrieved).
- [ ] Deliverables contain no guarantee language about rankings, citations or AI mentions.
- [ ] Traditional SEO health (crawl, index, content, links) is assessed alongside AI-specific checks.
- [ ] Findings are tied to evidence (screenshots, dated prompt logs, exports), not opinion.

## Actions
- Define scope (platforms, markets, business type) before auditing. [Foundation]
- Open ch01 for the five visibility signals and ch03 for the 30-day execution plan. [Quick win]
- Select the playbook matching business model (local, e-commerce, B2B, SaaS, publisher). [Foundation]

## Pitfalls
- Treating "AI SEO" as separate from classic SEO.
- Chasing every new AI platform instead of a repeatable measured process.
- Using the words platform and system interchangeably in reports; it blurs what the client can influence.
