# ch18 — Owning the Topic

## Core idea
- Treat visibility as territory, not a single ranking. Ask "where does my brand appear when someone learns, compares, validates, or decides?" rather than "do I rank?"
- Ownership = people, search engines and AI associate a topic with your brand, built from four factors: Coverage (questions, use cases, objections, comparisons), Consistency (aligned message), Credibility (others mention/cite/review you), Community trust (recognized as the solver of a specific problem).
- Goal is a footprint, not a page: easy to retrieve, verify, and remember. AI answer surfaces can combine a comparison page, review profile and support article into one answer, even if none ranks first.

## Blanketing SERPs and answer surfaces
- Answer surface = interface that generates a direct answer (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, AI Overviews). SERP = list of results.
- Blanket = cover a topic from multiple useful angles and formats, not duplicate content.

### Query types to cover
Definition | Problem-aware | Solution-aware | Comparison | Best-of | Cost/pricing | Review and alternative | Local/service | FAQ and objection.
- Example (bookkeeping software for freelancers): what it does; why freelancers struggle tracking expenses; how it helps; QuickBooks vs FreshBooks; best tools; cost; QuickBooks alternatives; services near me; "is a spreadsheet enough?"
- Coverage helps classic SEO and gives AI clean passages to cite for differently phrased questions.

### Asset formats
- Core landing page, supporting blog post, YouTube video, comparison page, downloadable checklist, founder interview, review profile, guest article, podcast appearance.

### SERP blanket process (per topic cluster)
1. Pick ONE priority topic tied to revenue, authority or positioning (not 20).
2. List search intents: awareness, comparison, validation, conversion.
3. Map one asset per intent (pricing = landing page; how-to = post/video; credibility = case studies/reviews).
4. Inspect live SERPs for core term and supporting terms (pricing, alternatives, reviews, use cases, who-it's-for) via autocomplete, People Also Ask, related searches, AI follow-up questions, pages that rank or get cited.
5. Fill gaps: SERP favors video and you have none; alternatives queries show only competitors; third-party mentions dominate (so earn visibility off-site).

### Avoid cannibalization
- Assign a distinct job to each asset: pillar = main commercial/educational intent; supporting articles = subquestions; comparison pages = decision stage; videos = explain/demonstrate; downloads = reusable tools.
- Two pages with the same job: merge, redirect, or reposition one.

## Owned assets as the narrative base
- Owned: website, email list, YouTube channel, podcast, LinkedIn newsletter, downloadable resources. Stable home base while platforms and algorithms shift.
- Topic hub on the site: main pillar/service page, supporting educational content, proof (reviews, case studies, testimonials), clear internal links, consistent point of view, current facts. Visitor should quickly grasp what you do, who it's for, why trust you.
- Repurpose by adapting the angle, not copying: pillar (full framework) -> blog post -> short video -> long YouTube video -> podcast segment -> email -> LinkedIn post (one mistake) -> checklist -> webinar -> FAQ page.
- Messaging sheet per core topic: positioning line, top claims, proof points, preferred terminology, 3-5 phrases to associate with the brand. Use it when creating, reviewing, repurposing content.

## Other People's Audiences (OPAs)
- OPAs = platforms, publishers, creators, experts, communities with existing attention. Borrow trust and distribution.
- Candidates: industry podcasts, niche newsletters, trade publications, YouTube channels, expert roundups, partner webinars, conference talks, guest articles.
- Three-test scoring: audience fit (overlaps ideal buyer), topical fit (reinforces topic), trust fit (credible host). Skip anything failing these.
- Contribution angles (give, don't ask for features): original data, tested framework, defensible bold point of view, case study, process breakdown, tactical checklist, implementation lessons.
- Turn one appearance into many signals: quote on site, social clip, blog recap, email, transcript excerpt, link on press page, reference in future pitches. Compounding mentions across surfaces, not one mention, builds ownership.

## Communities
- Forums, subreddits, Slack, Discord, Facebook groups, industry comment sections confirm ownership by human peer validation and shape what is repeated later. Some are partially visible to search/AI; all influence perception and language.
- Behave like a human: answer clearly, share examples, explain trade-offs honestly, mention your brand only when relevant, link only when it truly helps, participate consistently.
- Listen as research: confusion, vocabulary, recurring objections, competitors named, emotional pain points, persistent myths. Then rewrite content in the audience's words (lead with their phrasing, then introduce the technical term).
- Payoffs: direct traffic, brand searches, private recommendations, newsletter/blog mentions, speaking/collab invites, audience insight. Slower than spikes but longer lasting.

## Consistent signals: pick what to own
- Choose a deliberate association (topic + angle), e.g. "AI SEO for agencies", "emergency plumbing in West County", "fractional CFO for SaaS". Ask: what problem, which cluster, what angle/philosophy, what proof.
- Repeat on purpose: category, audience, point of view, proof, terminology, signature frameworks.

## Measuring ownership (monthly scorecard)
- Visibility: organic rankings, AI answers and citations, video search presence, branded search growth, third-party mentions, review-site visibility.
- Coverage: key subtopics covered, intents with dedicated assets, formats published, third-party placements.
- Trust: reviews, testimonials, community mentions, podcast invitations, partnership requests, backlinks and unlinked mentions.
- Grade coverage, consistency, credibility, community presence for one cluster per month; a simple sheet beats an unused dashboard.

## Ten signs of ownership
Brand+topic searches; ranking for multiple related queries; more AI mentions/citations; third parties reference your ideas; presence in roundups/comparisons; organic community mentions; prospects say they see you everywhere; easier positioning; more partnership/speaking invites; competitors reacting to your messaging.

## Templates & prompts
- Intent-to-asset map: query type | example query | asset | format | URL | status | owner.
- OPA scorecard: opportunity | audience fit (1-5) | topical fit (1-5) | trust fit (1-5) | contribution angle | status.
- Topic ownership plan (7 steps): choose one cluster -> list intents -> build owned hub -> add formats -> pursue 3-5 OPAs -> join 2-3 communities (help first) -> monthly footprint review.
- Messaging sheet fields: positioning line | top claims | proof points | preferred terms | 3-5 association phrases.

## Audit checks
- [ ] One priority topic cluster is defined with a stated association.
- [ ] Each of the nine query types has a dedicated asset or a deliberate decision to skip.
- [ ] Each asset has a distinct job; no two pages target the identical intent.
- [ ] A pillar page plus internal-linked supporting content and proof exists on the site.
- [ ] Live SERP and AI answers for the topic were reviewed; gaps (video, alternatives, third-party) noted.
- [ ] Multiple formats exist (page, post, video, comparison, checklist) for the topic.
- [ ] A messaging sheet exists and site, YouTube, bios use consistent claims/terminology.
- [ ] Brand appears on 3-5 relevant third-party audiences (podcasts, newsletters, roundups, publications).
- [ ] Brand has genuine, non-promotional presence in 2-3 communities.
- [ ] Past appearances were repurposed on site/social.
- [ ] A monthly scorecard (visibility, coverage, trust) is maintained.
- [ ] Not all proof lives only on the brand's own domain.

## Actions
- Pick one topic cluster and define the association to own. [Foundation]
- Map the nine query types to assets and fill the biggest gaps. [Foundation]
- Merge/redirect/reposition cannibalizing pages. [Quick win]
- Write the messaging sheet and align site, channel, and bio copy to it. [Quick win]
- Turn the pillar into video, email, LinkedIn, checklist, FAQ versions with different angles. [Ongoing]
- Score OPAs on audience/topic/trust fit and pitch with original data or frameworks. [Ongoing]
- Join communities, answer questions first, mine threads for language and objections. [Ongoing]
- Repurpose every appearance into several assets. [Ongoing]
- Review the scorecard monthly. [Ongoing]

## Pitfalls
- Random content unrelated to the chosen association.
- Treating the website as the whole game (no earned/third-party proof).
- Chasing every platform instead of where the market discovers and validates.
- Changing offers, claims, bios, and positioning constantly.
- Publishing near-duplicate articles for one keyword.
- Pitching OPAs with self-promotion; accepting irrelevant but impressive-looking audiences.
- Dropping links and self-promotion in communities.
- Starting with 20 topics.
