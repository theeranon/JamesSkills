# ch19 — Garnering Reviews That Count

## Core idea
- Reviews influence both conversions and AI/search confidence in mentioning a brand. Weak handling (inconsistent asks, wrong platforms, ignored negatives, volume over quality) creates a weak reputation footprint.
- Method: choose 1-3 platforms that matter, audit and clean the current footprint, run a steady request workflow, respond well, and use the reviews as feedback.
- Relevance beats ubiquity: ten strong reviews on the right platform can outweigh 50 on the wrong one.

## Why platform choice matters
- Buyers and AI compare platforms and look for consistent outside signals.
- Broad platforms (Google) show active/local/trusted at a glance; industry sites show category credibility and peer comparison. A software buyer may trust a detailed G2 review more than Google; a local customer checks Google Maps first.
- Mixed signals hurt: a strong Google profile with an empty industry profile, or fresh reviews on one site with three-year-old reviews elsewhere, looks stale.

## Matching platforms to business type
| Business | Likely primary / secondary |
|---|---|
| Local | Google Business Profile first; Yelp and local/niche directories |
| SaaS | G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, app marketplaces |
| E-commerce | Google, Amazon, retailer-specific review systems |
| Professional services | Google plus category sites: Avvo, Clutch, Healthgrades, Houzz or similar |
- How to find the right ones: search the service like a customer and note which platforms repeatedly appear with active reviews. Pick 1-3. If unsure, start with the one most visible in branded search and most trusted in the category.

## Audit the current review footprint (before asking for more)
- For each important profile record: review count, average rating, recency, level of detail, profile completeness, brand consistency (name/address/positioning), response activity.
- Fix gaps first (stale activity, outdated description, inconsistent name/NAP); adding reviews to a messy profile amplifies the mess.

## Platform priority list
- Primary: top 1-3 platforms. Secondary: worth maintaining. Low priority: keep accurate, don't push.
- Send most happy customers to the primary; rotate to secondary only with a specific reason. One clear destination reduces friction.
- (Chapter 16 guidance for local: ~80-90% of effort on GBP.)

## Steady review system
- A healthy profile looks active, recent, natural: steady flow beats bursts.
- Trigger moments: successful appointment, product delivered, onboarding completed, support issue resolved, positive private feedback. Ask shortly after a clear win ("earn the ask before you make the ask"): dentist after a smooth visit; SaaS after first strong result; agency after launch or reporting milestone.
- Workflow answers four questions: who asks, when, where customers go, how to follow up.
- Steps: 1 choose trigger; 2 assign responsibility; 3 send request (email/text/in person/in app); 4 link directly to the right review page; 5 send one polite follow-up if needed; 6 track requests and results.
- Embed into operations: CRM, onboarding, support follow-up, appointment completion sequence, milestone checklist. Don't rely on memory.
- Team rules: ask after positive outcomes; never pressure; send to the right platform; thank customers whether or not they review.

## Writing better requests
- Weak: self-serving, star-focused ("Please leave us a five-star review").
- Strong: warm thanks, optional ask, notes that details help others decide.
- Prompt for specifics without scripting: the problem they wanted solved, what stood out, the result, who they'd recommend you to. Never tell them what to write.
- Detailed reviews (problem + experience + outcome) are more persuasive, believable, and useful for visibility than "Great service".

## Banned shortcuts
- Fake reviews, review gating (screening to invite only happy customers publicly and routing unhappy ones privately), bribes, pressure, any platform-rule violation. They damage trust fast and can violate rules.

## Responding to reviews
- Positive: short, warm, human; use the reviewer's name and mention their specific feedback; avoid "Thank you for your five-star review."
- Negative: acknowledge concern, stay professional, don't argue details publicly, offer a next step (contact office/direct channel). Write for future readers, not only the reviewer.
- Fake or misleading: report to the platform; optionally reply calmly saying you can't identify the experience and inviting direct contact; don't accuse or overreact.
- Avoid robotic template replies.

## Improving review quality and using reviews as feedback
- Levers: ask at better times, encourage specifics, deliver a better experience (most important).
- Mine reviews for patterns: praised traits (friendliness, clarity, speed, expertise) -> feature in messaging; repeated complaints (communication, delays, confusion) -> fix operations and update public content (see ch16 sentiment, ch12, ch14).

## Templates & prompts
- Review audit table: platform | count | avg rating | newest review date | detail level (low/med/high) | profile complete (Y/N) | brand consistent (Y/N) | owner replies (% of reviews) | tier (primary/secondary/low).
- Request template: "Thanks again for trusting us. If you're open to it, we'd appreciate a review. Sharing a few details about your experience can help others make a good decision. [direct link]"
- Positive reply pattern: thanks + name + specific detail they mentioned + appreciation.
- Negative reply pattern: thanks for feedback -> sorry experience fell short -> take seriously -> contact [channel] to look into it.
- Request log columns: customer | trigger | date asked | platform | follow-up sent | review received (Y/N).

## Audit checks
- [ ] 1-3 primary review platforms are identified and justified by buyer behavior.
- [ ] Primary platform has recent reviews (not all old) and a steady rate rather than a single burst.
- [ ] Reviews include specifics (service, problem, outcome), not mostly one-liners.
- [ ] Average rating, count, and recency are recorded per platform.
- [ ] Profiles are complete: description, category, website link, images, contact, services.
- [ ] Business name, address, positioning match across review platforms.
- [ ] Owner responses exist for recent positive and negative reviews, and are non-templated.
- [ ] Negative responses are professional and offer a next step.
- [ ] A documented request workflow exists (trigger, owner, channel, direct link, one follow-up, tracking).
- [ ] Customers get one clear link, not a menu of platforms.
- [ ] No evidence of review gating, incentives, scripted text, or fake reviews.
- [ ] A review-theme analysis (praise and complaint patterns) has been done and fed into site content/operations.
- [ ] Secondary platforms (niche directories) are maintained, not abandoned.

## Actions
- Identify primary/secondary/low-priority platforms by searching like a customer. [Quick win]
- Audit each profile and fix completeness/consistency gaps before pushing traffic. [Quick win]
- Reply to unanswered recent reviews with specific, human replies. [Quick win]
- Define triggers and build the review request into CRM/onboarding/support sequences. [Foundation]
- Create direct-link request templates with prompts for specifics. [Foundation]
- Train staff on timing, no-pressure, and no-gating rules. [Foundation]
- Report fake reviews to the platform with a calm public reply. [Quick win]
- Run a monthly review-theme analysis and feed findings to marketing and operations. [Ongoing]
- Maintain a steady request cadence and track results. [Ongoing]

## Pitfalls
- Chasing quantity over relevance; pushing reviews everywhere.
- Letting reviews go stale.
- Giving customers too many platform options.
- Robotic template replies; arguing publicly with negative reviewers.
- Gating, bribing, scripting, or faking reviews.
- Asking before the customer has experienced value.
- Adding reviews to inconsistent, outdated profiles.
