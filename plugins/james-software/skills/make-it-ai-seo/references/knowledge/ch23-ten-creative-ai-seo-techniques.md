# ch23 — Ten Creative AI SEO Techniques

## Core idea
- After the site is crawlable, indexable, retrievable and trustworthy, add assets AI systems can interpret, summarize, reuse, verify and cite. Not tricks: make the best information easier to find, quote, verify.
- Principle: content with handles, written for humans first. If it only exists for a machine, rethink it.
- Ten techniques below: citation-worthy assets, fact hub, consensus kit, faster refreshes, public datasets, FAQs everywhere, change log, MCP/approved context, trust network, subject matter expertise.

## 1. Citation-worthy assets
- Formats: short definitions, concise answer blocks, bulleted takeaways, comparison tables, labeled statistics, quote-worthy statements, step-by-step instructions, mini glossaries, pros/cons lists, "best for" fit statements.
- Quick upgrades to existing pages: summary sentences near the top; split long paragraphs under labeled subheads; stat callouts; subheads phrased like real questions; comparison tables; "who this is for" section on commercial pages; key takeaways block.

## 2. Fact hub
- A public source-of-truth page for who you are and what you do. Contents: official name, short and long description, founding date, founders/leadership, HQ or service area, product/service categories, target audience, pricing basics, official brand spelling and name variants, policies/standards, media assets, contact details, milestones.
- Why: AI answers merge many sources; conflicting bios, directories, or old podcast blurbs cause confusion. The hub also keeps the team consistent across sales pages, bios, press, review profiles, partner copy.

## 3. Consensus kit
- A fact hub states what is true; a consensus kit helps the market repeat the same story with evidence.
- Contents: one-sentence positioning, longer description, category definition, founder/expert quotes, customer results and testimonials, case studies, product screenshots, benchmark data, methodology notes, review highlights, media mentions, comparison points, approved stats, objection-and-answer pairs.
- Audience: customers, reviewers, journalists, partners, influencers, AI systems. Same claim supported across credible sources gives AI more context to describe the brand accurately.

## 4. Refresh assets faster
- Priority pages (trust, visibility, revenue): home, about, pricing, product, service, comparison, case studies, top blog posts, help center, methodology, benchmark, review/reputation pages.
- Staleness signals: outdated screenshots, old dates, broken links, discontinued features, old pricing, outdated claims, missing recent product updates, references to old competitors, stats without a year, examples that no longer fit.
- Treat refreshes as continuous maintenance, signaling an active brand.

## 5. Public datasets
- Original evidence is hard to fake and copy. Need not be huge, just useful, clear, trustworthy.
- Examples: annual benchmarks, survey results, pricing studies, local market data, platform comparison data, trend reports, original experiments, anonymized customer insights, curated statistics pages, rankings with transparent methodology.
- Illustrations: agency benchmark of AI visibility across 50 local service firms; SaaS anonymized usage trends; consultant study comparing how AI platforms answer one category of question.
- Include a methodology note, date, and sample size.

## 6. FAQs sprinkled across the site
- Put FAQs where the question arises, not on one orphan page: product, service, pricing, comparison, location, category, onboarding, support, landing, case study, demo pages.
- Answer quality: direct answer in the first sentence; context only if needed; plain language; specific, no vague claims; link to deeper resources; short enough to scan. No disguised sales pitch.

## 7. Public change log
- Shows what changed, when, why it matters; supports freshness and trust signals. Useful for SaaS, apps, marketplaces, evolving agencies, education, content-heavy and research brands.
- Entry fields: date, short title, what changed, why it matters, related link if useful. Example pattern: "[Date]: Added Gemini tracking. [One sentence on user benefit]."

## 8. MCPs and approved context for automation
- Model Context Protocol: a permissioned way for AI tools to reach approved sources (docs, product data, content libraries, project tools, analytics, databases) instead of guessing from pasted scraps.
- Tiny sites with no automation can skip formal MCP; use the principle: define approved context sources (fact hub, help center, product docs, change log, SOPs, brand guidelines, structured datasets, customer success notes, approved descriptions) and instruct AI workflows to use them first.
- Example: instead of "write a product description," instruct "write from the fact hub, product docs, change log, and approved positioning."

## 9. Trust network
- The set of pages, profiles, mentions, reviews and references supporting credibility. Components: about page, author bios, expert profiles, case studies, testimonials, review profiles, directory listings, local citations, press, podcasts, partner pages, associations, certifications, awards, help center, methodology pages, social profiles.
- Goal: corroboration, consistent story across them. Conflicts make the brand look messy.

## 10. Subject matter expertise
- Sources: founders, consultants, technicians, product specialists, analysts, customer success, sales, experienced clients, partners, operators.
- Convert to assets: expert quotes, Q&As, reviewer notes, annotated articles, methodology explanations, short videos, interview posts, opinion pieces, comparison commentary, troubleshooting guides, "what most people get wrong" sections.
- Value: captures exceptions, mistakes, objections and "it depends" cases generic AI content omits.

## Templates & prompts
- Fact hub checklist: fields in technique 2 as a form.
- Change log entry: Date | Title | What changed | Why it matters | Link.
- Refresh triage table: URL | type | last verified date | staleness signal | owner | next review.
- SME interview prompts: What do beginners get wrong? What exception changes the answer? What would you tell a buyer comparing options? What data do you see that others do not?
- Context-bound AI prompt: "Using only the attached fact hub, docs and change log, draft [asset]. Flag any claim that is not in the sources."

## Audit checks
- [ ] Key pages open with a short direct answer or summary and use question-style subheads.
- [ ] At least one comparison table or labeled stat block exists on commercial pages.
- [ ] A public fact hub exists and matches LinkedIn, directories, and profile data (name, description, dates, locations).
- [ ] Brand name variants and official spellings are documented.
- [ ] A consensus kit (positioning, case studies, approved stats) exists and is shared with partners and PR.
- [ ] Priority pages show a current date and have no outdated screenshots, pricing, or broken links.
- [ ] Stats on the site carry a year and source.
- [ ] An original dataset, benchmark, or study with methodology is published.
- [ ] FAQs appear on product, service, pricing, and location pages, with answers starting with the direct answer.
- [ ] A public change log exists (where product or service changes) with dated entries.
- [ ] AI workflows draw from documented approved sources.
- [ ] Trust network elements (author bios, reviews, directory listings, certifications) are present and consistent.
- [ ] Expert-authored or expert-reviewed content is visible, with credentials.

## Actions
- Add summaries, key takeaways, and question subheads to top ten pages. [Quick win]
- Add comparison tables and "who this is for" blocks to commercial pages. [Quick win]
- Fix stale screenshots, pricing, dates, broken links on priority pages. [Quick win]
- Build the fact hub and align all external profiles to it. [Foundation]
- Assemble the consensus kit from existing case studies and approved stats. [Foundation]
- Publish one dataset with a methodology note. [Foundation]
- Distribute FAQs to relevant pages, replacing the single FAQ page approach. [Foundation]
- Launch a change log and write entries on each release. [Ongoing]
- Define approved context sources for AI workflows. [Foundation]
- Run SME interviews on a schedule and publish quotes and Q&As. [Ongoing]
- Run a quarterly refresh sweep. [Ongoing]

## Pitfalls
- Building machine-only content with no human value.
- Fact hub that contradicts other profiles or goes stale.
- Datasets without methodology or dates.
- FAQs that are sales pitches with question marks.
- Letting AI automations pull from unverified or pasted context.
