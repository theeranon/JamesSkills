# ch24 — AI Search Strategy 2027 for the Thai market

Source: Siripong Klinkajorn (ไอซ์ ศิริพงษ์ กลิ่นขจร), The Missing Piece, talk deck "AI Search Strategy 2027", November 2026, 79 slides. Market figures are the speaker's citations of StatCounter and Google; citation figures come from the speaker's own tracker (The Mention Lab). Both are source claims, not independently verified here. The talk also sells a course and the tracker; read the framework on its merits.

## Core idea
- The game moved from Keyword, Ranking, Click to Prompt, Mention, Citation. Buyers now describe their situation to an AI, let it compare options, then ask where to buy; the AI picks the information that best matches the need, not the biggest brand.
- A brand wins when many independent sources say the same thing about it (own site, reviews, media, community, video) and its own pages answer the question in their opening lines.
- Answers vary run to run, so visibility is a share across many repeated asks, never one screenshot.
- Work in a loop: find the real questions, check how AI talks about you, fix the page or the fact first, make other sources repeat it, then keep measuring.

## Thai market facts (source claims, dated)
- Search: Google holds about 99.4% of search in Thailand, Bing about 0.5% (StatCounter, Sep 2026). Classic Google SEO still carries most demand.
- AI chatbots by traffic share in Thailand (StatCounter, Oct 2026): Gemini 57.3%, ChatGPT 31.7%, Perplexity 7.8%, Claude 2.3%, Copilot 1.0%. Gemini overtook ChatGPT in Oct 2026. Traffic share is not user count.
- Implication: in Thailand, plan for Google's AI surfaces and Gemini first, ChatGPT second, and be present in every engine because the buyer's engine is unknown.
- Google's own AI Mode data (US, May 2026, cited by the speaker): AI Mode queries average three times the length of classic queries; "which" questions grew about 40% faster than overall queries; planning questions about 80% faster. Buyers ask longer, comparative, plan-shaped questions.

## What AI engines cite in Thailand (tracker study, source claim)
Study frame: 77,674 answers, 453,147 cited links, 2,300+ questions, 15 industries, five engines (Google AI Overviews, AI Mode, ChatGPT with search, Gemini, Perplexity), Thai settings, 10 Oct to 18 Nov 2026.
- Share of cited links that also sit in Google's top 10 for the same question: AI Overviews 38.1%, AI Mode 8.5%, Gemini 6.4%, Perplexity 2.3%, ChatGPT 1.4%. Ranking on page one feeds AI Overviews; ChatGPT and Perplexity cite well beyond it. Google states AI Overviews surface information "backed up by top web results".
- Of every 100 cited links: about 55 are the brands' or providers' own sites, 9 social (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok), 8 media and blogs, 6 government and institutions, 2 comparison or review sites, 2 Pantip and communities, 2 Google properties, 1 YouTube, 15 other.
- Brand-site share varies by industry: construction materials 67%, SEO and AI-search courses 58%, insurance 56%, banking 55%, beauty clinics 48%, hospitals 45%, real estate 39%, EV 26%, hotels 14%. The lower the share, the more the industry is decided off the brand's site (media, reviews, social).
- Engines differ: Google's AI Overviews cite social heavily (27% of links), AI Mode 15%; ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity almost never cite social. ChatGPT leans on government and institutional sites (13%); Gemini and Perplexity on media (12%). Gemini cites brand sites most (68%).
- Facebook is the most cited non-brand domain in every industry measured.
- The mix was stable over six weeks: brand sites about 60%, social creeping from 6.6% to 8.4%.
- Language: Google AI Overviews cite Thai sites 91% of the time, AI Mode 86%, Gemini 84%, ChatGPT 70%, Perplexity 65%. ChatGPT and Perplexity pull roughly one link in three from foreign-language sites, so English pages matter for them.
- Repeat the same question and the first-named brand stays the same only about 3 times in 10 on AI Overviews, Gemini and AI Mode, 5 in 10 on ChatGPT and 6 in 10 on Perplexity. One ask proves nothing.

## Conference consensus (Chiang Mai SEO 2026, speaker-reported claims)
- Brand mentions correlate with being cited more than link metrics do (an analysis of about 50 million answers).
- Answers drift about ±10 points across 100 repeated answers; collect many answers per question before concluding.
- About 29% of the passages AI cites come from the first 50 words of a page: answer at the top.
- Being first in comparison lists from several sources raised the mention rate from about 26% to 38%.
- "Make it true. Then make it repeated." Five steps: Discover (the buyer's real questions), Diagnose (how AI describes you now), Design (content that answers exactly), Distribute (many sources saying the same thing), Defend (monitor and correct continuously). Most teams stop before Defend.
- Every criticism an AI repeats about you is one task: fix the product or the fact first, then get sources to say the corrected version.
- SEO stops at the click; the business measures revenue. Group pages by job (money pages, knowledge pages, campaign pages), change one thing at a time, compare with untouched pages of the same kind over 28 days, and answer a lead within five minutes on the channel they used.

## Strategy 2027 in five steps
1. Decide where to win: one niche and its core customers before any content.
2. Find the questions those customers ask, as Scenario, then Topic, then Prompt: one short keyword (for example "SEO course") splits into scenarios (a beginner learning alone, a marketer upskilling for AI search, a team lead buying training for a team), each scenario into topics (where to start, price and value, certificates, choosing a trainer), each topic into the real prompts people type. Pick the scenarios to win first and track those prompts daily.
3. Track daily: ask the same questions automatically on each engine (the speaker runs four engines twice a day), record who is named, which sites are cited and how the share moves.
4. Make the site ready with the 27 checks below (Discover, Understand, Trust).
5. Loop with AI: feed Search Console, GA4, Microsoft Clarity, the mention tracker, brand documents and the site to an AI; it checks and recommends every day, a person decides, the AI drafts and ships under review, and results return to a shared brand memory so the next round is sharper (the speaker calls it the Signal-to-Strategy Loop).

## Discover: can AI find and read the site? (6 checks)
- robots.txt lets the search and AI crawlers in (Google, Bing, ChatGPT, Perplexity).
- No firewall or CDN rule turns AI crawlers away.
- Main content arrives in the HTML with the page, not after JavaScript or a click. Check: View Page Source shows the H1 and the first sentence.
- Important pages are indexed in both Google and Bing.
- Prices, conditions, how to order and contact details are text on the page, not images or PDFs.
- Pages open directly: no redirect chains, no noindex.

## Understand: can AI lift an answer from the page? (14 checks)
1. Page job in one sentence: which question it answers, for whom, and what to do next.
2. Page type matches what AI cites for that question (comparison, product, FAQ, guide).
3. The first two or three sentences answer the question.
4. Every criterion a buyer uses to decide has an answer on the page.
5. Subheadings are the real questions buyers ask.
6. Names are explicit (brand, product), never "here" or "this one".
7. When there are options, a real HTML comparison table, not an image.
8. Evidence sits next to each claim (number, review, certification) with its source.
9. A real FAQ: buyers' own questions, first sentence answers, at most three sentences.
10. Facts are correct and consistent everywhere on the page (prices, conditions, specs).
11. Freshness: an updated date; prices and offers current.
12. Media is described: images captioned, videos transcribed.
13. Internal links lead to the next decision page (product, price, contact).
14. Something only you can say: your own data or experience others cannot copy.

## Trust: does AI believe you are who you say? (7 checks)
1. Brand name, description and business category are written the same way everywhere.
2. An About page (and founders where relevant) with structured data that matches the page.
3. Mentioned, with the same story, in the sources people use to decide.
4. PR, social and the website tell the one story you want AI to repeat.
5. Real reviews on the platforms customers use (Google, Facebook, Pantip in Thailand).
6. A presence on YouTube: your own channel and other people's videos about you.
7. Verified business profiles (Google Business Profile and the engines' business listings) that match the site.

## Actions
- For ChatGPT and Perplexity, do not wait for a Google ranking: they cite far beyond page one, and about a third of their links are foreign-language, so publish crawlable English versions of the key pages.
- For Google AI Overviews, classic page-one SEO and an active Facebook presence both feed citations.
- Turn each recurring AI criticism into one fix: product or fact first, then the page, then the sources.
- Measure pages by job and by revenue, one change at a time, against untouched pages of the same kind over 28 days; answer leads within five minutes.

## Pitfalls
- Concluding from one answer: first-named brands repeat only 3 to 6 times in 10.
- Chasing links over mentions: mentions travel further in AI answers.
- Treating the tracker study as universal: it is one vendor's Thai sample for Oct and Nov 2026; recheck before quoting figures to a client.
- Copying the founder emphasis blindly: an About page with founders is a trust check, but a brand may choose a company-first identity; record that choice instead of overriding it.
