# ch22 — Ten Common AI SEO Myths

## Core idea
- Search is changing, not vanishing. Traditional SEO remains the base; AI SEO adds new surfaces (answers, citations, mentions, comparisons).
- Rule of thumb for the agent: expansion, not replacement; one shared foundation plus per-platform adjustments; judge by evidence and usefulness rather than hype or panic.
- Use this file as a claim-checker: map the claim to a myth, apply the corrected rule and action.

## Myth 1: Google is dead
- Reality: Google remains a main discovery engine for products, services, comparisons, reviews, pricing, local, tutorials. AI Overviews and AI Mode sit on top of search behavior and rely on web content and authority signals similar to classic inputs.
- Rule: keep investing in high-intent search content; monitor where AI changes click paths; treat Google as part of the AI SEO ecosystem.

## Myth 2: Traditional SEO is dead
- Reality: what is dying is lazy SEO (keyword stuffing, spun articles, junk links). Machines still need something to retrieve, interpret, summarize, trust.
- Foundations that stay: clear architecture, crawlable pages, indexable content, internal links, strong titles/headings, helpful content, relevant authority, fast accessible pages, consistent brand information.

## Myth 3: AI SEO is just SEO
- Reality: overlap is large, but AI SEO adds visibility work: being mentioned, cited, included, compared, or excluded inside generated answers; entity consistency; share of voice in AI answers; off-site corroboration.
- Rule: keep SEO baseline and add AI-answer tracking, entity/fact consistency, and third-party signals.

## Myth 4: Blocking AI crawlers is always smart
- Reality: blocking can protect licensing leverage, proprietary research, privacy, or legal positions, but broad blocking can make you undiscoverable and uncitable.
- Decision rule by content type:
  - Public product guides, service pages, FAQs, educational articles: usually allow.
  - Paid databases, gated research, members-only lessons, private docs: restrict (with real authentication).
- Decide per bot and per section with a documented reason, not a blanket reflex.

## Myth 5: You can "rank" in ChatGPT
- Reality: answers are assembled dynamically; output varies with prompt wording, available sources, retrieval settings, geography, personalization, memory, and product updates. There is no stable position number.
- Better metric questions: how often does the brand appear, in what context, for which prompts? Track by prompt set, repeated runs, and mention/citation presence.

## Myth 6: Longer content is better for AI
- Reality: clear, complete, well-structured content wins; padding hurts. A 4,000-word article saying what 900 words could is a defect.
- Favor: good organization, scannability, clear sections, facts and examples, concision where possible, depth where needed, descriptive headings, evidence or experience. Length should be an outcome of coverage, never a target.

## Myth 7: AI-generated content gets penalized
- Reality: low-quality, unreviewed content is the problem, not AI assistance itself. Both extremes are wrong: avoiding AI out of fear, or publishing raw output.
- Poor use: prompt for a 2,000-word "best CRM" article, publish unchecked, no testing, no price verification, no experience.
- Good use: AI draft plus product testing notes, screenshots, pricing checks, expert commentary, examples, and editorial review. Test: did a responsible human own the final content?

## Myth 8: Your website is the only asset that matters
- Reality: the site is the source of truth you control, but AI systems corroborate across third-party mentions, review platforms, directories, local citations, social profiles, podcasts, press, community discussion, and knowledge-graph-style references.
- Rule: build the off-site footprint; audit third-party profiles for consistency.

## Myth 9: Backlinks don't matter
- Reality: authority now includes more than links, but links still help rankings, trust, and discovery. Keep terms distinct:
  - Backlink: clickable link from another site.
  - Mention: reference to brand/product/person/site, linked or not.
  - Citation: source an AI system uses or displays to support an answer.
- A credible article linking to your guide can help rankings, brand trust, and AI discoverability; a review-site mention can influence which brands appear even if the link is imperfect; a citation can create visibility with no click.

## Myth 10: One universal strategy works on every platform
- Reality: Google AI features, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude and future tools differ in sources, interfaces, citation handling, personalization, and integrations.
- Strategy shape: one shared foundation plus platform adaptations discovered by observing each system.
- Shared foundation: strong technical SEO; clear topical coverage; factual structure; off-site credibility; consistent entity signals; useful trustworthy content; reviews and proof; authority from relevant sources.
- Platform layer: record which sources and formats each engine cites, then adapt.

## Audit checks
- [ ] Strategy documents do not assume Google traffic is irrelevant; Google organic is still tracked.
- [ ] Core technical foundations (crawl, index, internal links, titles, speed, consistent NAP/brand data) are verified, not skipped for "AI-only" work.
- [ ] The measurement plan tracks AI mentions/citations by prompt set, not a single "ChatGPT rank."
- [ ] Crawler rules are intentional per bot and section; public valuable pages are not blanket-blocked, restricted content is truly protected.
- [ ] Pages are judged on structure and completeness, not word count targets.
- [ ] AI-assisted pages show human-added evidence (testing, pricing checks, expert input, review) before publishing.
- [ ] The off-site footprint (reviews, directories, press, social, local citations) is audited, not only the website.
- [ ] Backlinks, mentions, and citations are reported as three separate metrics.
- [ ] The plan has a shared foundation plus documented per-platform notes.
- [ ] No deliverable promises fixed AI ranks or guaranteed citations.

## Actions
- Replace "rank in ChatGPT" KPIs with prompt-level appearance rate, context, and citation presence. [Quick win]
- Review robots.txt and bot rules against the open/restrict rule by content type. [Quick win]
- Trim padded pages; restructure with descriptive headings rather than lengthening. [Foundation]
- Add a human-evidence checklist to AI-assisted drafts. [Foundation]
- Add off-site profile and mention audits to the standard scope. [Foundation]
- Maintain a per-platform observation log (sources cited, formats favored). [Ongoing]

## Pitfalls
- Overreacting in either direction: abandoning SEO or ignoring AI surfaces.
- Believing one engine's behavior generalizes to others.
- Treating blocked crawlers as a protection strategy while still publishing the content publicly.
- Padding for length; treating AI output as publish-ready.
