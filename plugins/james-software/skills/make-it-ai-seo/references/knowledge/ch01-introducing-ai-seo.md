# ch01 — Introducing AI SEO

## Core idea
- Traditional SEO helps pages show up; AI SEO helps the brand become part of the answer (mention, citation, shortlist inclusion, accurate description).
- A site can rank yet be absent when a user asks an AI tool what to buy, whom to hire or what to trust.
- AI SEO sits on top of traditional SEO: crawlable pages, clear content, topical authority, reviews and links still matter.

## What AI SEO adds on top of traditional SEO
- Retrieval readiness: key pages and individual passages are easy to find, parse and cite.
- Entity clarity: brand, products, people, locations, categories are described consistently on-site and across the web.
- Answer visibility: track whether AI systems mention, cite, compare, shortlist or ignore the brand for relevant prompts.
- Off-site consensus: reviews, profiles, roundups, communities, podcasts, directories, press all repeat the same story as the website.
- Responsible publishing: verify claims, get permission for testimonials/screenshots/reviews, human-review AI-written content.

## The five signals AI systems weigh (use as the master audit frame)
- Discoverability: can systems find important pages, profiles, reviews, proof assets?
- Clarity: is it obvious who you are, what you offer, who you serve, what differs?
- Consistency: do site, profiles, reviews and mentions repeat the same core facts (name, offer, location, pricing, category)?
- Credibility: do trusted sources, customers, experts and data back the claims?
- Freshness: are pricing, product details, screenshots, policies, examples, stats current?

## How an answer is assembled (simple model)
- Retrieve candidate sources (passages, facts, reviews, profiles) -> read and synthesize -> optionally cite or link sources -> platform decides display.
- The "evidence layer" is everything that feeds this: website, profiles, reviews, citations, community presence, media coverage, original data.
- The website is the home base and source of truth, but not the whole game: Google Business Profile, LinkedIn, review sites, directories, podcasts, Reddit, press, author bios and comparison articles all shape the description. Conflicts between them make the brand hard to summarize.

## Who needs what (page-type emphasis by business model)
- Local: be named for nearby service, emergency and "best in city" prompts.
- E-commerce: products, categories, reviews, shipping details, buying guides for comparison.
- B2B: proof, industry pages, case studies, expert signals to get onto buyer shortlists.
- SaaS: pricing, features, comparison, alternatives, security, integrations, onboarding pages.
- Publishers: trustworthy structured content that earns citations and converts visibility into audience ownership (direct followers/subscribers/visits not dependent on third-party platforms).

## Visibility outcomes to aim for (pick at least one per project)
- Brand appears more often for commercial prompts.
- AI describes the brand accurately.
- Own site or trusted third-party sources get cited more.
- Strongest pages are easy to retrieve and cite.
- Off-site footprint makes positioning more credible.

## Quick-start plan (5 steps)
1. Establish baseline: choose 10-20 customer-style prompts (include "best", "top", "near me", "alternatives", "versus", "pricing", "review", "for [audience]"). Record platform, exact prompt, date, brand appeared?, where in answer, own site or third party cited?, accuracy/sentiment (accurate, positive, neutral, wrong). This is the Day 0 view.
2. Fix source-of-truth pages (home, About, Contact, pricing/cost, product/service, FAQs, policies, proof pages, decision-stage pages). They must answer: who are you; what do you offer; who is it for; where do you operate; what does it cost / how pricing works; what proof supports claims; what happens next after buying/booking/contacting.
3. Make best pages retrievable: direct answer near top; headings matching real questions; short stand-alone passages; comparison tables where choices matter; FAQs where objections appear; specific proof (examples, screenshots, data, reviews); internal links to the next-question pages.
4. Build off-site consensus: clean Google Business Profile and local directories (local), LinkedIn/Crunchbase/social/founder bios, review platforms (Capterra, Clutch, G2, Trustpilot, Yelp, niche), roundups/listicles/alternatives pages/podcasts/press/partner pages, communities where buyers compare options.
5. Track, test, adjust: measure coverage, prominence, share of voice, citation quality, accuracy and sentiment; adjust based on evidence.

## Diagnostic decision rules
- Site cited but brand not named -> proof or positioning is weak.
- Competitors appear because review/listicle sites mention them and not you -> third-party evidence gap.
- Brand appears on one platform but not another -> platform-specific testing needed.
- Conflicting facts across site/profiles -> fix the official page first, then update profiles and third-party sources that matter most.

## Audit checks
- [ ] Home, About, Contact, pricing, product/service pages answer the seven source-of-truth questions.
- [ ] Each key page opens with a direct answer (2-3 sentences) near the top.
- [ ] Headings mirror real customer questions.
- [ ] Comparison tables exist where buyers choose between options.
- [ ] Core facts (name, description, category, location, pricing) are identical on site and top profiles.
- [ ] Pricing/policy/product details and statistics are current (dated or recently reviewed).
- [ ] Brand has a presence on the review/directory platforms relevant to its model.
- [ ] A Day 0 prompt baseline (10-20 prompts) exists with dated screenshots.
- [ ] Internal links connect key pages to their next-question pages.
- [ ] Testimonials, screenshots, reviews used have permission and are verifiable.
- [ ] Reporting includes more than traffic (coverage, position, SoV, citations, accuracy, sentiment).
- [ ] Searching brand and category in classic search and AI tools shows no conflicting descriptions.

## Actions
- Run the 10-20 prompt baseline across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot, Google AI Mode/AI Overviews. [Quick win]
- Rewrite source-of-truth pages to answer the seven questions explicitly. [Foundation]
- Add a direct-answer paragraph, question headings, FAQs and a comparison table to top pages. [Quick win]
- Reconcile brand facts across all profiles to match the website. [Foundation]
- Fill review-site and directory gaps relevant to the business model. [Ongoing]
- Set a recurring measurement routine (coverage, SoV, citations, accuracy). [Ongoing]

## Pitfalls
- Treating AI SEO as a magic trick (secret prompt, hidden schema property, one-click tool). Tools track visibility; they do not create reputation or proof.
- Declaring traditional SEO dead and skipping technical SEO, content quality, internal links, topical coverage, accessibility, speed, mentions.
- Measuring only traffic: AI answers influence decisions pre-click; buyers may return via brand search or direct.
- Letting conflicting facts persist (pricing page vs review site vs old podcast bio vs LinkedIn category).
- Publishing faster than governance: sloppy comparison claims, unauthorized testimonials, stale pricing screenshots, unsupported health/financial statements spread widely.
