# ch20 — Earning Links, Mentions, and Citations

## Core idea
- Three separate evidence signals: a link (clickable pointer to your site), a mention (brand named, linked or not), a citation (source an AI answer uses or shows). They work together; relevance beats volume.
- A few references on credible, on-topic pages outperform many weak links. Goal is corroboration that your brand belongs in its category, not trophy-collecting.
- Lead with expertise and useful assets, not "please add a link."

## Target decision-making pages first
- Highest-impact off-site targets are pages where buyers compare and choose: best-of lists, top-tools roundups, alternatives pages, niche directories, local best-in-city pages, curated resource pages.
- They matter twice: humans use them to decide; search engines and AI systems read repeated third-party appearances as proof of category membership.
- Discovery queries to run (search engine and AI assistants):
  - best [category] / top [category] / [category] alternatives
  - best [category] for [audience]
  - best [service] in [city] (local); top [industry] companies; [category] tools; [category] roundup
  - SaaS: "[competitor] alternatives"; services: evaluator-style pages and niche directories.
- Prioritize pages that are: highly relevant, written for buyers, editorially credible, maintained/updated, aligned with your positioning.
- Skip thin, off-topic, or pay-to-place pages. Filter test: would you want to be listed there if there were zero SEO value? If no, drop it.

## Make your brand easy to include
- Editors have no time to decode you. Prepare: one-sentence company description, who it is for, key proof points, accurate pricing or ranges (if relevant), visuals/screenshots, and a clean page explaining the offer.

## Target tracking sheet
- One spreadsheet, columns: target page, site, contact, page type, why you belong, outreach angle, priority, status, live link or mention, notes.
- Status flow suggestion: identified, angle chosen, contacted, followed up, won (link/mention/citation), lost/skip.

## Expertise-led digital PR
- Weak outreach asks for a link; strong outreach contributes something quotable. Useful contributions: expert commentary, original data, a practical framework, a strong point of view, a real example, a clear explanation of a trend or common mistake.
- Source of expertise: client work, campaigns run, product building, patterns seen repeatedly. Package as quotable content or data. Example framing: "we saw this pattern across N campaigns, here are three takeaways."
- Pitch-angle types: surprising insight, contrarian opinion, simple framework, timely reaction, repeated mistake, practical checklist, short case lesson.
- Pitch structure (4 parts): why the topic matters now; the insight you offer; why you are qualified; what you can supply quickly (quote, data point, example).
- Keep it short, personal, tied to the specific article or creator.

## PR kit (reusable, so you are low-friction to cite)
- Founder/company bio, company boilerplate, headshot/logo, topics you can speak on, approved proof points/stats, a few prewritten quotes.
- Value is speed and accuracy: writers do not have to chase you.

## Link-worthy assets by business type
- Local: local guides, local statistics pages, neighborhood resources, seasonal homeowner checklists.
- SaaS: original studies, benchmark reports, calculators, free tools, templates.
- Agencies/consultants: audit templates, frameworks, scorecards, implementation checklists.
- General examples: study, stats page, free tool, calculator, checklist, framework, benchmark, template.
- Test: is it a reference tool people reach for when they need evidence, examples, or a shortcut to a decision?
- Make it easy to cite: clear title, main takeaway at top, summary bullets, visuals/tables, quotable findings, short methodology if data is involved.

## Promotion channels (publishing alone is not enough)
- Targeted outreach to writers who covered the topic, pointing to one specific stat or finding.
- Industry communities (short takeaway plus discussion question).
- Newsletter editors who curate resources.
- Social posts built from one chart/quote linking back.
- Podcast/webinar/interview mentions as supporting evidence.
- Existing relationships: partners, customers, vendors.
- One asset can feed multiple outreach angles.

## Weekly operating system
1. Find 5 opportunities: 2 decision-making pages, 2 expert-commentary/PR chances, 1 asset-promotion chance.
2. Pick one angle per opportunity (why you belong or what you offer).
3. Send focused outreach.
4. Follow up once or twice: first reminder 3-5 business days later, final one about a week after, only if the opportunity is valuable.
5. Log who, what pitched, follow-up dates, outcome (link / citation / mention / reply).
6. Repeat winning page types, angles, and assets.

## Tactics to avoid, and walk-away rules
- Avoid: undisclosed paid links, mass link exchanges, private blog networks, spammy guest posts, irrelevant directory submissions, low-quality AI filler built for links.
- Walk away when: site is off topic, page is low quality, audience mismatch, placement would confuse the brand, site obviously sells placements, or you would be embarrassed to show it to a prospect.

## Templates & prompts
- Pitch skeleton: "Hi [Name], I saw your roundup on [topic]. Across [number/type of cases] we have seen [specific trend]; one takeaway for your readers: [one-sentence insight]. I can send a short quote, the data point, or a quick example."
- Discovery prompt for AI assistants: "What are the best [category] for [audience] in [city/market]?" then record which sources the answer cites; those cited sources are outreach targets.
- Tracking columns: see sheet above.

## Audit checks
- [ ] A list of decision-making pages (best-of, alternatives, directories) exists for the main category and each money query.
- [ ] Brand appears on the top-ranking best-of/alternatives pages for its category queries (check manually and in AI answers).
- [ ] Sources cited by AI assistants for category prompts are identified, and brand presence on each is known.
- [ ] A tracking sheet with status per target exists and is current.
- [ ] A one-sentence description, proof points, and pricing summary are published on a clean offer page.
- [ ] A PR kit (bio, boilerplate, logo, quotes, approved stats) is accessible at a public or shareable URL.
- [ ] At least one link-worthy asset (study, tool, benchmark, template) exists and has a methodology note.
- [ ] Referring domains are topically relevant; no obvious PBN/paid-link/irrelevant-directory patterns in the backlink profile.
- [ ] Mentions without links are logged, and unlinked mentions on strong sites have been followed up.
- [ ] Outreach follows a cadence (max ~2 follow-ups) and results are logged.

## Actions
- Build the target list from category, alternatives, audience, and city queries plus AI-cited sources. [Foundation]
- Create the tracking spreadsheet and score targets with the "would I want it without SEO value" filter. [Quick win]
- Write a one-sentence positioning line and publish a clean offer/pricing page for editors. [Quick win]
- Assemble the PR kit and host it on the site. [Quick win]
- Choose one asset type matched to business model and publish it with title, takeaway, bullets, and methodology. [Foundation]
- Run the weekly 5-opportunity loop. [Ongoing]
- Disavow-or-ignore review: stop any engagement with link sellers and list-farms. [Ongoing]
- Reuse each winning pitch angle across similar publications. [Ongoing]

## Pitfalls
- Chasing link counts over relevance; accepting any directory.
- Generic "add us to your article" outreach with no value offered.
- Publishing a data asset without methodology (not citable).
- Skipping the tracking sheet, so wins and failures teach nothing.
- Treating a mention as worthless because it has no link.
