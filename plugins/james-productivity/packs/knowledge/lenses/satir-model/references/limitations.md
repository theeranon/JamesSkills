# Limitations

## SM-201 — Nothing here is evidence-based, in any setting

- Evidence: `[independent evidence]`
- Source: `satir-model-1991#ch5`
- Confidence: `source-faithful`

There is no meta-analysis, no Cochrane review, and no pooled effect size. The model is absent from the APA Division 12 list, the California Evidence-Based Clearinghouse, NICE guidance, and the serial reviews that established the family-therapy evidence base. That absence is specific to this model: the field around it is evidence-based, and several named family-therapy models clear the bar.

About six trials exist, all confounded, most using wait-list or no-treatment controls with self-report outcomes and author-built instruments. The most informative of them found self-esteem improved while drug craving, treatment motivation, and family functioning all came back null. A family model failed to move family functioning.

The 2016 review that examined the change process model found four includable studies, two of them unpublished theses, and warned that the work risks being forgotten for want of evidence. The most sympathetic contemporary reviewer, who is affiliated with a Satir institute, states in her own thesis that the studies lack robust methods such as randomised trials.

**Any claim that this model is validated or proven is unsupported as of September 2026.** It is a descriptive heuristic with a rich vocabulary. Say that plainly rather than softening it.

## SM-202 — The iceberg is entirely unmeasured

- Evidence: `[independent evidence]`
- Source: `banmen-satir-yesterday-today-2002#fig1`
- Confidence: `source-faithful`

No published instrument measures any of the eight levels. The stance scales that exist are unpublished theses, one validated on people who had attended Satir workshops, others whose own authors describe the reliability as not satisfactory. There is no independent confirmatory factor analysis establishing a four-stance structure in any international peer-reviewed journal.

Never present a stance reading as a measurement.

## SM-203 — The agile change model is not Satir's

- Evidence: `[independent evidence]`
- Source: `satir-model-1991#ch5`
- Confidence: `source-faithful`

The version circulating in software and agile came through Gerald Weinberg, whose 1997 volume on anticipating change carries a chapter on it, and then through a widely copied web article that renamed the second stage to resistance, made the model about groups, and added a performance axis. Four specific breaks from the original:

1. The original is intrapsychic and family-based, not organisational.
2. The foreign element is inverted. In the book the therapist is the foreign element, a chosen intervention. In the software version it is an imposed reorg.
3. The performance curve is probably not Satir's at all. A LeSS co-creator looked for it in her book and did not find it, arguing it should be called the Weinberg curve and separated from the model.
4. A peer-reviewed software paper has already mis-cited the 1991 book for a five-stage list that appears only on the web.

No Satir body has endorsed, criticised, or even acknowledged the software version. Its institutes' bibliographies contain nothing on software, agile, or engineering.

When someone cites the Satir Change Model in a work context, name whose rendering they are using.

## SM-204 — Structural criticism worth stating

- Evidence: `[independent evidence]`
- Source: `satir-model-1991#fig-iceberg`
- Confidence: `supported`

The method is hard to separate from the practitioner. There is no manual and no fidelity measure, which is the standard critique of experiential family therapy. Yearnings, life energy, and Self are not operationalised anywhere. A philosophical appreciation hosted by one of the institutes itself notes limits to the iceberg metaphor and warns that the life-force idea needs critical examination.

A 2021 feminist critique found the model lacks an explicit lens on power across race, gender, and sexual orientation, and that while Satir handled gendered power in her own practice, the transmissible model does not teach how.

Downstream, the most commercially successful thing extracted from her method is neuro-linguistic programming, which is classified as pseudoscience.

## SM-205 — Not validated for these, specifically

- Evidence: `[independent evidence]`
- Source: `satir-model-1991#ch5`
- Confidence: `source-faithful`

Trauma and PTSD: effectively no evidence, and institutes publish trauma material carrying no outcome data and no practitioner-qualification statement. Clinical depression: never compared head to head with an established treatment. Obsessive-compulsive disorder: zero studies found. Personality assessment: contradicted by the model's own literature. Organisational performance and executive coaching: no published outcome study exists at all.

No Satir body publishes a warning that iceberg exploration may surface trauma, or any guidance on when a non-clinical practitioner must stop. That gap is why the referral rule in `applications.md` comes from coaching-profession guidance instead.

Do not repeat the claim that congruent leadership produces thirty-one percent lower turnover. It is attributed to a business review and is untraceable to any study.

## Evidence boundary

What this lens may claim: that the model offers a useful vocabulary and a workable question sequence, and that named practitioners have applied it to coaching and leadership.

What it may never claim: that it is validated, evidence-based, proven, or measured. That any stance reading is an assessment. That any stage list of the change process comes from Satir. That the model predicts organisational performance.

Every reading it produces is a hypothesis about one moment under stress, dated, held loosely, and dropped when it stops changing the question.

## Prohibited uses

- Personality typing, hiring, promotion, pay, or team assignment.
- Diagnosis, trauma processing, or continued work with a person whose daily functioning is impaired.
- Describing this lens, or any skill built on it, as Satir certified, official, accredited, or endorsed. It is none of those.
- Reproducing the 1991 book's text or diagrams, any institute's paid question booklet, or any assessment instrument.
- Presenting the software or agile change curve as Satir's own work.
- Repeating the untraceable turnover statistic, or any other figure without a locator.
