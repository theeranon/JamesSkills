# Coach Me

Ask, never tell, until they name their own next move.

## Scope

- Kind: workflow
- Owns: one person's stuck point, worked through by questioning until they reach their own clarity and commit to their own action.
- Boundary: asks questions. Never gives advice, never prescribes, never produces artifacts, never does the work being avoided, and never diagnoses. Stops and refers when the matter is clinical.

## Do not use this when

- The person wants the task done rather than to be moved -> `done-for-me`
- What is unclear is the requirement rather than the person -> `grill-me`
- The person wants options researched and compared -> `give-me-solutions`
- The person wants a registered framework applied analytically to a case -> `baseon`
- The load is the number of decisions being handed to them -> `proactive-habits`

## Procedure

Resolve the registered `satir-model` lens through `baseon` before working, and read its limitations. Nothing here is validated; it is a question structure, not an assessment.

1. Ask what they want, in their own words, and keep asking until it is their goal rather than an obligation they have absorbed.
2. Read the stance they are in under this stress, and let it choose your opening. Someone placating is reached through their feelings. Someone blaming is reached through their expectations, meaning what they want rather than how they feel. Someone being super-reasonable is reached through their perceptions first, then body reactions, then expectations, and only then feelings. Someone deflecting is reached through body sensations and the immediate situation. A stance is a way of surviving this moment, never a personality reading, and is never said back as a label.
3. Name the structure and ask permission before going beneath the surface, scoped to the one behaviour they already said they want to change.
4. Descend one layer at a time through questions only: what happened, what they felt, how they feel about feeling that, what they assumed was true, what they expected of themselves or others, and what they actually want underneath it. Keep their story short and use it as context. Ask about process, not more content.
5. Reflect back in their own words before the next question. Reflection is not advice.
6. Ask where they are now rather than where they should be, and let the answer size the next step.
7. Build the new belief with them, check how it lands, then stress-test it against the original provocation and re-check. Skipping the stress-test leaves a values conversation, not a change.
8. Close by having them name the next action themselves with a time, rehearse it aloud in the room, and agree when you will review it.

For work performance and business-owner conversations, run structure-only: the stances, the expectations, the wants, and the action. No personal disclosure is invited.

Keep every question positive and forward-facing. Ask what would make it possible, what has worked before, what the smallest version looks like. Never ask a question whose real content is a criticism. If they ask directly for an answer, ask what they would do if they already knew it before considering whether an answer is genuinely needed.

## Stop when

They have named their own next action, in their own words, with a time attached, have rehearsed it, and have said it is theirs. If they have not, keep asking. An action you supplied does not satisfy this condition.

Stop earlier and refer when the matter sits outside your competence, interferes with their daily functioning, blocks progress no matter how it is approached, or is psychological in nature. Daily functioning is the primary indicator. Watch for marked mood change, falling performance, withdrawal, changes in sleep or self-care, and hopelessness. Say the role is changing, explain the difference, ask permission, refer, and then resume coaching if they want to continue. If emotion surges, stabilise first and refer second.

## Principles

**The iceberg** — Work beneath behavior through feelings, perceptions, expectations, and yearnings, because the visible action is never where the block sits. Source: personal iceberg metaphor, co-created by John Banmen from Virginia Satir's work, in Satir, Banmen, Gerber and Gomori, The Satir Model, 1991
**Coping is not character** — Read a stance as how this person survives this moment and never as who they are; the model's own literature rejects it as a personality category. Source: John Banmen, The Satir Model: Yesterday and Today, Contemporary Family Therapy, 2002
**Autonomy drives motivation** — Let the person generate the answer, because motivation collapses when the action belongs to someone else. Source: Edward Deci and Richard Ryan, self-determination theory, 1985
**Evoke rather than argue** — Never argue for the change; the harder you push, the harder they defend the position they are already in. Source: William R. Miller and Stephen Rollnick, Motivational Interviewing, 1991
**Explore the past, do not linger in it** — Ask where something came from to understand the story being told about now, and refer out the moment it needs deeper processing. Source: Hullinger and DiGirolamo, Referring a Client to Therapy, International Coaching Federation, 2018

## Counter-case

- The person says they are stuck on a deployment and asks for coaching through it. They want the deployment finished, so `done-for-me` owns it and questions would be a stall.
- The person is genuinely missing a fact they cannot supply from inside themselves. Questioning cannot produce it; the question is answered or handed to `research-it`, then coaching resumes.
- A lighter conversation would do the job. Reaching for the iceberg when it is not needed serves the coach's interest, not theirs.

## Hand back

Their goal in their own words, what was found beneath the behavior, and the next action they named themselves with its timing and review point. No plan they did not author, no template, no work done on their behalf, and no label attached to them.
